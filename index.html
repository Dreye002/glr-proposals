How to Compare Roof Quotes

The bottom number rarely tells the whole story. The details do.

Two roof quotes can look almost identical on the bottom line and be completely different once you look at what's actually included. The goal here isn't to rush you, it's to make sure you're comparing apples to apples before you choose anyone. Run both quotes through this list.

Check each one on BOTH quotes

What to compareWhy it matters
Same roof area / squaresOne quote may measure less roof than the other.
Tear-off vs. layoverGoing over an old roof is cheaper now, costlier later.
Underlayment typeThe waterproof layer under your shingles. Quality varies a lot.
Flashing (new vs. reused)Old reused flashing is a common leak point.
VentilationPoor ventilation shortens roof life and can void warranties.
Decking / plywood assumptionsIs bad wood replacement included, or a surprise add-on later?
Permit handlingIs the permit pulled and included, or left to you?
Cleanup & magnetic nail sweepProtects your yard, pets, and tires.
Photo documentationDo you get photos of the work, or just take their word for it?
Warranty pathWorkmanship AND manufacturer material warranty, in writing.
Change-order termsWhat happens, and what it costs, if they find more once they start.
License & insuranceLicensed, bonded, insured, and verifiable.
The cheapest quote often wins by leaving things out. If one number is much lower, this list usually shows you why, and it's rarely good news once the roof is off.
What cheaper quotes often leave out: full tear-off (vs. roofing over old layers), new underlayment, new flashing, proper attic ventilation, permits, dump fees, and what happens if they find bad decking once they start. The bottom number rarely tells the whole story. The details do.

Understanding Your Roof Warranty

Workmanship and material coverage, in plain English.

Roof warranties confuse almost everyone, and that's where some contractors get away with weak coverage. Here's what actually matters, in plain English.

The two warranties on every roof

TypeWhat it covers
Workmanship warrantyThe installation, the labor, how the roof was put on. This comes from the roofer.
Manufacturer material warrantyThe shingles/material themselves. This comes from the manufacturer (like GAF).

Why the installed system matters

The strongest manufacturer warranties are only available when the full system is installed correctly by a certified contractor. A cheap install can actually void the material warranty, so the lowest bid can cost you coverage.

Why ventilation and flashing matter long-term

Poor ventilation traps heat and moisture and shortens roof life. Reused or sloppy flashing is one of the most common leak points. Both affect whether your roof, and your warranty, actually last.

Your exact warranty depends on the roof system you choose. That's why it's worth walking through carefully rather than guessing, your Green Ladder rep can explain exactly what applies to your proposal.

Questions to ask any roofer

  • Is the workmanship warranty in writing, and for how long?
  • What manufacturer warranty does this system qualify for?
  • Are you certified to offer that manufacturer warranty?
  • Does anything I'm choosing affect the coverage?

Buy With Confidence

5 Questions to Ask Any Roofer

Bring these to any roofer, including us.

A roof is one of the biggest investments you'll make in your home, and not every company is upfront about the things that matter most. Here are five questions worth asking any roofer before you commit, whether you go with us or not.

1. Are you licensed, bonded, and insured, and can you prove it?

Ask for the license number and a certificate of insurance. A real company hands these over without hesitation. If they dodge, that tells you something.

2. What's in the scope, line by line?

Underlayment, flashing, ventilation, tear-off, decking, permits, and cleanup should all be spelled out. Vague scopes are where change-orders and surprise costs hide.

3. What warranty do I actually get, and in writing?

There are two: the workmanship warranty (from the roofer) and the manufacturer material warranty (from the maker). Get both in writing, and ask if your install qualifies for the manufacturer's best coverage.

4. Will I get photos of the work?

Photo documentation means you can see what was done, not just take someone's word for it. It's also proof for insurance or a future sale. Ask if it's included.

5. What happens if you find more once you start?

Bad decking or hidden damage is common once the old roof comes off. Ask exactly how that's handled and what it costs, before it becomes a surprise.

Why we put this together: a confident, well-informed homeowner makes a better decision, and usually a calmer one. We'd rather you choose us because you understand the work, not because someone rushed you.
